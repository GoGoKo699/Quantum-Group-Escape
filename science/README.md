# Scientific reading route

Start with the distinction between a known group and an unknown representation. A random word is known classical randomness; the represented endpoint is the quantum operation that must be implemented on an arbitrary supplied input.

1. [Model, output and charged resources](model.md) fixes the exact representation promise, full-channel error and maximum serial query count.
2. [Group escape quantiles](group-quantiles.md) proves the general upper/lower bracket. Short words give the upper bound; a common parity-gauge conjugation preserves every group relation and gives the converse.
3. [Wreath-product escape](wreath-product.md) analyzes the cursor-and-integer-lamps example, deriving finite-time upper and lower escape quantiles from conditional lamp fluctuations and cursor occupation times.
4. [Finite quotients](finite-quotients.md) proves that explicit finite groups preserve the required metric ball, transfers the probability bounds and states the growing hard-instance width.
5. [Algorithms and resources](algorithms-and-resources.md) turns sampling and word compilation into capped procedures, allocates probability and implementation error, and prices classical work separately.
6. [Interpretation](interpretation.md) explains what the geometric principle establishes and how the output/access boundaries affect it.

[Related work](../references/related-work.md) supplies attribution and the closest model-level comparisons. The [finite verification](../verification/README.md) supports algebraic conventions and implementations; it is separate from the proofs.
